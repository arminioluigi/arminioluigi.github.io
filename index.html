About

I am a postdoctoral researcher at Roskilde University, where I am part of the Digital Media Lab. My area of expertise is computational social science, with a particular focus on communication dynamics within highly polarized debates on social media.

I am currently researching a new source of social media polarization: generative AI (GenAI). I approach this from a dual perspective. On one hand, I analyse how social media users react to GenAI, ranging from enthusiastic support to deep skepticism. On the other, I investigate how AI-generated content reshapes online communication. My research is funded by the SCAI and ADD projects.

I completed my PhD at the IT University of Copenhagen in 2026, where I was part of the Networks, Data, and Society (NERDS) group. My thesis studied polarization in the online climate debate, advancing both analytical and methodological contributions to the study of polarization in an evolving social media environment. I did this by combining recent methodological advancements in AI, such as transformer-based language models and vision-language models (VLMs), with more traditional NLP and statistical methods. Through this work, I developed a VLM-based pipeline for semantic clustering of visual content that groups images by their connotative meaning rather than merely what they depict, supporting large-scale analyses of public discourse in an increasingly visual social media environment.
